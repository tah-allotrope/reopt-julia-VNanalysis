## 1. Executive Summary

Cong's Session 6.2 slide deck (29 slides) is the strongest piece of technical content on Vietnam DPPA mechanics Allotrope has produced for a CEBA workshop audience. The five-line bill breakdown, CfD settlement math, developer economics, and case-study framing are thorough and pedagogically sound. However, the deck went into final review *just after* the most important event in Vietnam's DPPA history — the Samsung SEVT × TTC Duc Hue 2 deal going live on 1 June 2026 — and does not reflect it. It also misses two regulatory developments that materially change the buyer's calculus: Resolution 253 (December 2025) and the Decree 146 two-part tariff (Phase 3 pilot starting July 2026). Finally, there are several framing inconsistencies with Allotrope's own quantitative analysis of the Samsung deal that should be resolved before the HCMC workshop. The recommendations below are sequenced from highest to lowest priority.

## 2. What the Slide Covers Well

### 2.1 The Five-Line Bill (Slides 6–11)
The breakdown of the monthly DPPA settlement into five distinct cost lines — market energy, grid-service adder, transmission, distribution, and CfD settlement — is excellent. No other session in the workshop agenda explains this at this level. The numerical worked example (6,000 MWh/month at ~1,864 VND/kWh effective cost vs. ~2,204 VND avg retail) is concrete and memorable.

### 2.2 CfD Mechanics and the Two-Way Settlement (Slides 12–15)
The explanation of how a CfD creates price certainty rather than automatic savings is one of the most important concepts for a corporate audience who often arrive expecting a discount. Slide 14 ("Year 1 usually costs more than BAU") and the instruction to "evaluate on 10-year and lifetime cumulative cost — never on Year 1 alone" are key messages Allotrope should be proud to lead with.

### 2.3 Developer Economics and the Three-Gate Framework (Slides 17–19)
The framing of buyer, seller, and lender as three simultaneous gates (buyer premium/saving, developer IRR, DSCR ≥ 1.2x) is conceptually correct and maps directly onto how project finance actually works. The lifecycle cost items table (Slide 19) — listing O&M escalation, battery replacement cycles, grid-fee revision risk — is unusually detailed and valuable.

### 2.4 Pedagogical Structure
The modular roadmap (Slide 2) and the session's scope statement ("virtual/grid CfD DPPA only") are well-disciplined. The 8760-hour simulation methodology (Slide 21) signals technical rigor that will build credibility with sophisticated attendees.

### 2.5 Honest "Window is Empty" Conclusion (Slides 24–25)
Showing attendees that none of 56 strike/volume scenarios pass all three gates simultaneously is an important market reality check. The instruction to "ask developers HOW their project is financed, not just what they charge" is advice that will save workshop participants significant time in due diligence.

## 3. Where the Slide is Out of Date or Misaligned with Recent Developments

### 3.1 ⚠️ CRITICAL: No Reference to Vietnam's First DPPA Deal (Samsung SEVT × TTC, June 1, 2026)
**The most significant gap.** On 1 June 2026, Samsung Electronics Vietnam Thai Nguyen (SEVT) and TTC Duc Hue 2 Solar Power Plant became the first entities to operate under Vietnam's DPPA mechanism. Key deal facts:
- Generator: 49 MW Duc Hue 2 solar (Tây Ninh province, southern Vietnam)
- Buyer: SEVT smartphone factory, Yen Binh Industrial Park, Thai Nguyen (northern Vietnam)
- Distance: ~1,500 km — the definitional signature of a financial (not private-wire) DPPA
- Volume: ~70 GWh/yr, reducing CO₂ by ~46,000 tonnes/yr
- Certified by the National System and Market Operator as compliant with all technical and legal requirements under Decree 57

Cong's case study (Slides 21–25) uses a **hypothetical** "industrial park" buyer. The Samsung deal is now public, better understood, and far more compelling as an anchor case study. The workshop audience will ask about it regardless.

The slide's "window is empty" conclusion (Slide 24) is accurate for a generic developer at conservative assumptions, but Allotrope's own internal analysis of the Samsung deal (DPPA insights.pptx) shows the **buyer saves ~25 B VND/yr (~$0.95M)** at the regulated ceiling strike. This nuance is entirely missing from Cong's deck.

### 3.2 ⚠️ CRITICAL: Two-Part Tariff Risk Absent (Decree 146 / Decree 57 amendments)
Allotrope's own analysis (DPPA insights local run, Slide 9) identifies the Decree 146 two-part tariff as a critical sensitivity: the capacity charge **could lift Samsung's EVN bill by ~18% (+365 B VND)**. Phase 3 of the two-part tariff pilot goes live **July 2026** — the same month as the HCMC workshop. This means every DPPA buyer in the room will be hearing about it on their actual invoices within weeks.

- **Phase 2 (Jan–June 2026):** Paper-based parallel invoices — buyers receive both old and new format bills but pay only the old tariff.
- **Phase 3 (July 2026 → July 2027):** Official pilot — capacity charge hits actual bills for large industrial consumers.
- **Risk:** Double-charging DPPA volume under the capacity component (capacity charge is fixed regardless of whether renewable volume was delivered). This could erode buyer savings from a DPPA that looked favorable under the current tariff.

Session 6.2 currently contains **zero** mention of the two-part tariff. This should be at minimum a warning slide.

### 3.3 HIGH: Resolution 253 (December 2025) Not Mentioned
National Assembly Resolution 253/2025/QH15 (11 December 2025) removed the ceiling price constraint for **private-line (physical) DPPA**, allowing prices to be freely negotiated between buyer and seller. While Session 6.2 explicitly scopes to virtual/grid CfD DPPA where the ceiling may still apply, the resolution matters for two reasons:

1. Workshop attendees may be considering both physical and virtual models.
2. The removal of price caps for physical DPPA changes the competitive landscape: well-located corporates with land near industrial parks can now negotiate freely, shifting relative economics vs. the regulated virtual model.

### 3.4 MEDIUM: Strike Price Range Inconsistency with Allotrope's Own Analysis
Cong's case study sweeps strikes from **1,200–2,200 VND/kWh** (Slide 24). However, Allotrope's analysis of the Samsung deal anchors at the **Southern ground-mount ceiling of 1,012 VND/kWh** — which is meaningfully lower than Cong's floor. The Samsung deal appears to have been executed at or near this ceiling.

This creates a potential inconsistency: attendees who have heard about the Samsung deal (at ~1,012 VND/kWh) will hear Cong's case study starting at 1,200 VND/kWh and wonder if the numbers are comparable. A brief cross-reference note or recalibration of the case study anchor is needed.

### 3.5 MEDIUM: CBAM/EU Supply Chain Documentation Not Addressed
Multiple sources (EnergyTag, VinEnergo, TechTimes) highlight that Vietnam DPPA is now intersecting with CBAM (EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism) concerns. The workshop audience — primarily supply chain members of MNCs like Samsung, Apple, and Google suppliers — will increasingly need to document the additionality and temporal matching of their renewable energy claims under EU and Scope 2 reporting standards. The slide's framing of DPPA value ("price certainty plus credible renewable claims") should note that claim documentation quality matters — a DPPA paired with unbundled EACs from a generic portfolio does not satisfy CBAM requirements as well as a specific matched-generator arrangement.

### 3.6 MEDIUM: "No Project Examples Exist" Now Wrong
Allotrope's original template slide (Slide 6, DPPA insights original template) states "In early stages of adoption. No project examples exist to date." This is now factually incorrect. While Cong's session does not reproduce this exact language, the overall framing of the case study as purely hypothetical is outdated. The session should explicitly state that **one live deal exists** and that the case study parameters are calibrated to match it.

### 3.7 MEDIUM: Corporate DPPA Pipeline Only Implied
The broader pipeline of interested buyers — reportedly including Apple supplier manufacturers, Heineken, Google's supply chain, KN Holdings × Samsung C&T floating solar — is not mentioned. After a "window is empty" conclusion, the audience needs to understand that the market is still in early formation, not that the DPPA mechanism has stalled. This context matters for attendees considering multi-year procurement planning.

## 4. Conflicts with or Divergences from Allotrope's Own DPPA Narrative

### 4.1 The 523 VND/kWh Grid Service Adder: Mentioned vs. Dominant Lever
Cong's slide (Slide 11) presents the 523 VND/kWh grid-service adder as one line item: "They are set by regulation, not negotiation." Allotrope's internal analysis (DPPA insights local run, Slide 9) identifies this as **the single biggest swing factor on buyer cost** — larger than the strike itself within the regulated band. The buyer flips from saving to paying a premium near ~0.9× that adder. This finding should be elevated in Session 6.2, not buried.

Recommended framing: a dedicated sensitivity slide showing the buyer's annual position (saving vs. premium) as the adder moves from 0× to 2×, with the break-even clearly marked. This reframes the "523 VND/kWh is fixed" statement to "523 VND/kWh is your biggest fixed risk — calibrate it first."

### 4.2 Developer NPV: "Sub-Economic" Needs Context
Allotrope's analysis explicitly notes (DPPA insights local run, Slide 15): "For the generator (TTC): do not read 'uneconomic' from this screen — it is conservative (revenue on the contracted 70 GWh, not the plant's full ~92.5 GWh yield; $750/kW capex). Counting full output + a merchant tail would lift NPV."

Cong's "window is empty" conclusion does not carry this caveat. It risks giving attendees the impression that no deal can ever make sense for developers, when in fact the conservative screen excludes the merchant upside that is a real part of developer economics.

### 4.3 Path to Bankability: "Fix is Structural" Without Specifics
Slide 28 states: "if the window is empty, the fix is structural, not just price." This is correct. But Allotrope's internal analysis identifies the specific structural levers: lower leverage (60% vs. 70% debt), USD-denominated debt (~5% vs. ~8.5% VND), debt sculpting, fee relief, longer tenor. The panel discussion that follows (as referenced in Slide 28) presumably covers some of this, but the slide itself should at least list the three to four structural levers so attendees arrive at the panel with the right framework.

### 4.4 Framing of "Buyer-Favorable" Economics
Allotrope's most current analysis finds the Samsung deal **buyer-favorable** at the ceiling strike. Cong's hypothetical case study finds "zero of 56 scenarios pass all three gates." These conclusions are not contradictory — they reflect different scenarios (buyer-favorable ≠ deal viable for all three parties) — but the juxtaposition needs a bridge slide or clear verbal explanation to prevent confusion. Without it, attendees from the Samsung supply chain who attend both sessions may leave thinking Allotrope contradicts itself.

## 5. Specific Recommendations

The recommendations below are ordered by implementation priority for the HCMC workshop.

### 5.1 ADD: "Market Update" Opening Slide (Before Slide 1)
Insert a 1-slide "As of June 2026" market update immediately before or after the session roadmap:

> **Vietnam DPPA: Where We Are Now**
> - ✅ 1 Jun 2026: Samsung SEVT × TTC Duc Hue 2 goes live — Vietnam's first grid DPPA (49 MW, 70 GWh/yr)
> - ⚠️ Jul 2026: Two-part tariff Phase 3 pilot — capacity charge hits actual bills for industrial consumers
> - 📋 Dec 2025: Resolution 253 — ceiling price removed for physical (private-line) DPPA
> - 📊 Buyer pipeline: multiple MNCs (Heineken, Apple supply chain) reportedly preparing DPPA bids

This slide takes 2 minutes but completely reframes the session from "theoretical" to "this is happening right now."

### 5.2 ADD: Samsung-TTC as Case Study Anchor (Module 5 or new Module 0)
Replace or supplement the hypothetical "industrial park" case study with the Samsung SEVT × TTC deal parameters:
- Buyer: 22–110 kV, ~1,000 GWh/yr total load, DPPA slice = 70 GWh (~7%)
- Generator: 49 MWp, southern Vietnam (Tây Ninh), ~92.5 GWh total output
- Distance: ~1,500 km — virtual/financial DPPA
- Strike: anchored near the Southern ground-mount ceiling (~1,012 VND/kWh)
- Result: buyer saves ~25 B VND/yr at ceiling strike (directional); developer sub-economic on contracted slice alone

This makes the math tangible and directly relevant to the supply chain audience.

### 5.3 ADD: Two-Part Tariff Risk Slide (After Module 1 or as a Module 1.5)
Add a dedicated slide titled "The Variable You Must Model: The Two-Part Tariff." Key messages:
- Decree 146 pilot Phase 3 starts July 2026: your bill will now have a capacity component (VND/kW) separate from the energy component (VND/kWh)
- For a buyer like SEVT, this could raise the EVN bill by ~18%, increasing the DPPA's avoided-cost value — but only if the capacity charge doesn't also apply to DPPA-matched volume (double-charging risk)
- Action: ask your EVN account manager for a Decree 146 proforma invoice NOW, before signing a DPPA

### 5.4 REVISE: Grid-Service Adder Sensitivity (Elevate in Module 2)
In Slide 11, add a callout box: "The 523 VND/kWh grid-service adder is the dominant leverage on your total cost — larger than the strike itself. A doubling of this adder erases your savings. Source the project-specific wheeling fee before evaluating any term sheet."

Optionally add a mini-sensitivity bar chart showing buyer saving vs. premium at 0×, 1×, and 2× the adder.

### 5.5 REVISE: "Window is Empty" Conclusion — Add Path to Bankability (Slide 24/28)
After presenting the empty strike window, add 3–4 bullet points on what makes a window exist:
- Lower leverage (60% → LTV reduces debt service, widens developer window)
- USD-denominated debt (~5% vs. ~8.5% VND) — raises developer NPV significantly
- Full plant output counted (not just contracted slice): merchant tail revenue changes developer IRR materially
- Longer loan tenor (15 yr vs. 10 yr) smooths DSCR profile

This transforms a deflating conclusion into an actionable "here's how to work toward a viable deal."

### 5.6 REVISE: Add Developer Nuance on "Sub-Economic"
Add a note after the developer economics section: "A 'sub-economic' screen result on the contracted slice is not the whole picture — it excludes the merchant tail on uncontracted output (~92.5 GWh generated vs. 70 GWh contracted in the Samsung case). Experienced developers price in this merchant upside when setting the strike. Your negotiating counterpart already has."

### 5.7 ADD: CBAM/Supply Chain Documentation Note (Module 6 or Decision Points)
In the Decision Points module, add a question: "6. Can we document the renewable claim for EU CBAM and customer Scope 2 reporting?" Note that a DPPA paired with generator-specific hourly matching is more defensible than unbundled RECs for EU CBAM purposes — a growing concern for export-oriented manufacturers.

### 5.8 ALIGN: Strike Floor with Samsung Precedent
In Module 3 and the Case Study, reference 1,012 VND/kWh (Southern ground-mount ceiling) as the current market floor for CfD DPPA, not 1,200 VND/kWh. The Samsung deal sets this anchor and attendees will compare.
