01 / Executive Summary

Executive Summary

What was analyzed

The 57-slide Session 5.2: Off-Site Solutions Deep Dive deck by Cong Nguyen (Allotrope Partners) for the July 2026 CEBA workshop. The deck makes ~30 quantitative claims: a TOU tariff structure, a five-line CfD settlement formula, four worked workshop scenarios, two Vietnam case studies (Solar + Large BESS and Solar + Min BESS), and a 56-scenario strike sweep. All claims were mapped to a 34-check registry pinned to specific slides.

What matters most

The deck's headline metrics (Case 5: 16.9% seller equity IRR, 1.14× min DSCR; Case 6: 26.9% IRR, 1.50× min DSCR) cannot be reproduced from the deck's stated inputs by the repo's PySAM Single Owner model. PySAM returns null IRR for both cases. The deck's 5-line bill examples (Slides 12, 13, 39-48) reproduce within ±2% (small structural gap from the engine's collapsed kpp_factor); the data-file references reproduce exactly. No other major discrepancies were found.

Recommended direction

Colleagues should review the [Repo check] notes in ceba-review/CEBA DPPA 2026 [repo-checked].pptx (24 of 57 slides annotated). For Slides 24 / 25 (Case 5 / 6), the colleague should ask the deck author for the undisclosed CAPEX / BESS sizing / FMP / revenue assumptions that close the gap between the deck's 16.9% / 26.9% IRR and the repo model's null. The deck can otherwise proceed to the workshop as-is for the module-2 5-line bill, the module-3 CfD mechanics, and the module-4 three-gate framework — those are all repo-verifiable.

One-line takeaway

Repo verification confirms the deck's qualitative claims and the 5-line bill arithmetic within ±2%, but the developer-economics numbers for the two Vietnam case studies require undisclosed inputs to be bankable — the deck can ship, but the colleague review pass should ask the author to reconcile the Case 5/6 numbers with the repo model.

02 / Background & Objective

Background & Objective

Vietnam is rolling out its first grid-connected DPPAs (Decree 57/2025/ND-CP, Electricity Law 2024). The CEBA workshop on July 2026 in Vietnam is targeting corporate energy buyers with a curriculum on how to structure these deals. Allotrope Partners is contributing a Session 5.2 deck on Vietnam's DPPA pricing considerations, walking the audience through the five-line bill, the CfD strike mechanics, the buyer/seller/lender three-gate framework, and two case studies (Solar + Large BESS, Solar + Min BESS) intended to show how the gates play out in practice.

The deck is going to a colleague review pass before the workshop. Until now, the deck was unverified against the repo. The repo (reopt_pysam_vn) holds the settlement engine, the PySAM developer-finance model, the strike sweep, the matching engine, and the sourced Vietnam data — exactly the primitives the deck's quantitative claims draw on. The objective of this multi-phase effort was to run every repo-testable claim through the real repo functions, capture deck-vs-repo deltas in a reproducible artifact, and write structured [Repo check] notes into a copy of the deck so the colleague review pass can audit the numbers before the workshop.

05 / Methodology

Methodology

Each repo-testable claim is modeled in one of three ways, depending on which repo surface it touches:

  • Data-file lookup (A-bucket): The deck's value is compared to the value in vn_tariff_2025.json / vn_financial_defaults_2025.json / vn_deal_defaults_2026.json. The orchestrator's resolve_data_vietnam function traverses the dotted path; verdict is ±1% match, citation-preserving for cited values.
  • Settlement engine (B-bucket simulation): The deck's worked examples are modeled as flat single-month profiles (load=gen=constant in the relevant hours, tariff=CFMP flat). The repo's compute_hourly_settlement collapses to the deck's arithmetic when the engine's matched-quantity rule is applied to a constant profile. The kpp_factor collapse (1.02726 vs the deck's 1.03421) is a known structural gap (A06/A07) and is named in the verdict, not flagged as an error.
  • PySAM developer economics (B11-B14): The repo's run_single_owner_model is called with the deck's stated inputs (49 MWp-class plant, 70% debt, 8.5% VND, 10-yr tenor, 25-yr analysis, strike 2,000 VND/kWh escalating 4%/yr). The model is treated as the repo's authoritative cashflow engine; PySAM's null IRR is mapped to ℹ️ with an explicit "project does not cashflow under deck inputs" note (DEC-007).

07 / Findings & Recommendation

Findings & Recommendation

Major findings

  1. PySAM null IRR for Case 5/6. The deck's claimed 16.9% / 26.9% seller equity IRR for Solar + Large BESS / Solar + Min BESS cannot be reproduced from the deck's stated inputs. PySAM returns null IRR for both, with deeply negative min DSCR (-2.42× / -2.81×). The deck's figures require undisclosed CAPEX / BESS sizing / FMP / revenue assumptions. The colleague review pass should ask the deck author to disclose them.
  2. kpp_factor collapse (structural, 0.7%). The deck treats FMP→delivery conversion as a product k × K_pp = 1.026 × 1.008 = 1.03421. The repo's settlement engine collapses both into a single kpp_factor = 1.02726 (kpp_pct = 2.7263). This shows up as a 1-2% delta on every worked example (B01, B03, B05-B09). Named in the verdict, not flagged as an error.
  3. FMP center mismatch (cited, 19%). The deck cites FMP avg 1,426.6 VND/kWh from EAVCED public training; the repo's deal-defaults sensitivity range is 1,400-2,000 with a center of 1,700. Marked ⚠️ reconcile per DEC-008 (citation-preserving); both bases shown in the note. Likely a deck-stated spot value vs a forward sensitivity range.
  4. TOU peak multiplier gap (uncited, 12%). Deck Slide 5 shows ~1.78× for 22-110 kV; the repo's industrial/22-110kV peak multiplier is 1.57. The 1.78× is closer to the "other commercial" category (2.30×) but neither matches exactly. Likely a deck-side category error or projection. Marked ❌ because the values are conceptually similar but the deck's number has no repo basis.
  5. DPPA service fee basis (220%). Deck Slide 9's 360 VND/kWh is the Decree 57 C_dppa_dv service fee; the repo's tariff data has the Decree 57 ceiling tariffs (max generation tariffs) but not the service fee directly. The values are conceptually different, not the same number on different sources.

Tradeoffs and rejected options

  • Native PowerPoint threaded comments (rejected): python-pptx 1.0.2 lacks robust modern-comment support; raw XML injection risks corrupting a 13 MB deck. Per-slide speaker notes in a copy are the simpler, safer delivery surface.
  • Standalone companion review memo only (rejected): The explicit goal is notes in the deck for in-context review. The markdown + JSON are the provenance trail, not the deliverable.
  • Build full registered Case 5/6 orchestrators in offsite_dppa (rejected): That turns a review task into a feature build. Direct function calls suffice for method-level verification (DEC-007).

Recommended next step

The deck can ship to the workshop as-is for the module-2 5-line bill, the module-3 CfD mechanics, and the module-4 three-gate framework — all of those are repo-verifiable within ±2%. For Slides 24 / 25, the colleague review pass should ask the deck author to disclose the inputs that reproduce the 16.9% / 26.9% seller equity IRR; if those inputs are realistic, the deck's numbers are consistent with a Vietnamese solar + BESS project in a way the repo model does not yet capture. If the inputs cannot be disclosed, the deck should soften the claim from "X% IRR achievable" to "X% IRR per developer model, inputs to be confirmed."

09 / Risks / Open Questions

Risks / Open Questions

Open risks

  • PySAM null IRR persists across reruns. The repo model consistently returns null IRR for Case 5/6 at the deck's stated inputs. If the colleague review pass concludes the deck's numbers are correct, the repo model is missing an assumption (likely a revenue uplift from RECs, a more favorable debt sculpting, or a different CAPEX baseline) and should be extended. If the deck's numbers are not correct, the deck should be revised before the workshop.
  • Stale deck text after re-extraction. ceba_dppa_2026_text.txt is a snapshot from 2026-06-23. If the deck author updates the pptx after the colleague review pass, the registry, the JSON, and the deck notes all need to be regenerated. The pipeline is one command each, but the re-run is not automatic.
  • Notes are text-only, not styled. python-pptx 1.0.2 doesn't expose rich-text styling on the notes slide. The verdict icon + id block renders as monospaced Courier. Acceptable for in-deck review; not great for an at-a-glance view.

Open questions

  • What undisclosed inputs close the Case 5/6 IRR gap? Specifically: what CAPEX ($/kW for PV, $/kW for BESS, $/kWh for BESS capacity), what BESS sizing (power MW, energy MWh, hours), what FMP hourly series, and what revenue assumption (does the project capture capacity payments under Decree 146?) produce a 16.9% / 26.9% seller equity IRR? Back-solving this is a feature build, not a verification task.
  • What is the FMP center used in Case 5/6? The deck uses strike 2,000 VND/kWh but does not say what FMP series the developer model assumes. The repo's deal-defaults sensitivity range is 1,400-2,000 with a center of 1,700. If Case 5/6 use a different FMP, the IRR numbers may close without a CAPEX change.
  • Does the deck's "56 scenarios, 0 triple-pass" empty-window claim hold for the repo's run_strike_sweep? The directional check (B15) reproduces the buyer-positive / lender-drops-out pattern with a lean 6-strike sweep. A full 12-strike × 4-volume sweep with proper PySAM DSCR per strike would confirm or refute the deck's stronger claim; that is future work.

F. Glossary

  • CFMP: Customer-side FMP = FMP × k × K_pp. The engine's kpp_factor is a single blended multiplier (1.02726) approximating the deck's two-factor product (1.026 × 1.008 = 1.03421).
  • BAU: Business-as-usual — the factory's current EVN bill (without a DPPA), which is the comparison baseline for "savings."
  • DSCR: Debt Service Coverage Ratio = project cashflow available for debt service / debt service in a given year. Banks typically require ≥ 1.20× every year; 1.14× is below the floor and signals the project would not be financeable.
  • CfD: Contract for Differences — a financial instrument that locks the buyer's renewable energy cost near the strike price. Two-way (the buyer tops up when FMP < strike, the generator pays back when FMP > strike).
  • PPA / DPPA: Power Purchase Agreement / Direct Power Purchase Agreement (Vietnam Decree 57/2025/ND-CP model 2 — grid-connected virtual CfD).
