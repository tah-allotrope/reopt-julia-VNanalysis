01 / Executive Summary

Executive Summary

What was done

Three committed phases: remove both shim layers (3 src/ + 51 flat scripts/python/*.py), untrack ~35 MB of heavy reference binaries with provenance manifests, and slim activeContext.md from 2,117 → 55 lines. Each phase shipped to main with its own HTML report.

The headline lesson

"Compatibility shims" sounded cosmetic but were load-bearing. 7 of 51 flat scripts were real 115–404-line implementations (relocated, not deleted), and 7 tests imported the moved code through the shims. The full 217-test suite — not a subset — surfaced every break.

Result

One canonical src/python/reopt_pysam_vn/ package and one scripts/python/{reopt,pysam,integration}/ tree. Binaries git-ignored with recovery manifests. Suite: 215/217 integration pass; reopt 137 + ingestion 106 + pysam green.

Honesty note

Two integration tests remain red — proven pre-existing by re-running them at the pre-Sprint-2 commit (identical numbers). They are flagged as backlog, not introduced here. One self-inflicted .gitignore over-match was caught and corrected mid-sprint.

05 / Methodology

Methodology

Every flat script was classified by a simple rule: a canonical twin exists in a subdir AND the file is a thin (≤18-line) redirect ⇒ shim ⇒ delete; otherwise ⇒ real implementation ⇒ git mv into integration/. Binary removal was preceded by a reference grep and followed by provenance manifests. The decisive methodological choice was to run the entire integration suite after the structural move — a subset had given false confidence — and, when two numeric tests failed, to attribute cause rigorously by re-running them at the prior commit in a throwaway git worktree.

07 / Findings & Recommendation

Findings & Recommendation

Finding 1 — "Shim" is a dangerous label. 14% of the flat layer was real code, and the genuine shims were re-export points that tests imported through. A structural rename therefore touched test behavior. The mitigation that worked was mechanical classification plus a full-suite gate.

Finding 2 — Attribution matters as much as fixing. Two red tests could have been blamed on the refactor and "fixed" by chasing ghosts. Re-running them at the prior commit proved they were pre-existing benchmark drift, keeping the change honest and the scope tight.

Recommendation. Proceed to Sprint 3 (generalize the onsite/offsite pipelines) on the now-canonical layout, and separately schedule the 2 pre-existing benchmark failures as a small backlog fix. Keep the "full suite after structural moves" rule as standing practice.
