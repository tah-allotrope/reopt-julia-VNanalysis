Peak Shaving Strategy
Integrating an Energy Management System (EMS) and BESS is the defensive measure.
BESS detects impending capacity thresholds and automatically discharges to intervene.
Shaving the grid power draw profile helps factories avoid expensive, fixed monthly capacity penalties.

[Slide 9]
Electricity License Exemption (Decree 61):
Proposes complete exemption of generation licenses for BESS under 3 MW,  removing the biggest administrative bottleneck and enabling ultra-fast deployment of Behind-The-Meter (BTM) systems.
Loosened Export Caps (Decree 58):
Raises the ceiling for selling excess rooftop solar up to 50% of actual generation, providing a safety net for oversized systems during holidays and weekends.
Peer-to-Peer Trading (DPPA):
Paves the way for industrial parks to establish micro-grids, letting enterprises sell excess solar energy to partners within the same network.
Specialized National Standards (TCVN):
Over 15 new National Standards issued in sync with IEC 62619, resolving barriers related to fire safety and bank credit requirements.
Supportive Regulatory Framework (2025–2026)

Profile Risk:
If company
consumes a lot of power during the evening
(paying high retail rates because there is no solar) but
doesn't use enough at noon
(when solar power peaks and market prices crash), the factory will suffer a
"double loss"
→ Pay expensive
retail prices to EVN
and pay
out-of-pocket to cover the CfD losses
to the generator.
Curtailment Risk:
When the grid is congested, generation is curtailed by the NSMO. This mismatch forces the buyer to purchase 100% of their load at standard retail prices from EVN, causing a shortage of bundled Renewable Energy Certificates (RECs).
BESS - The Firming Solution:
Increased Matching Rate:
Co-located BESS discharges during the evening peak, significantly increasing the temporal alignment between RE generation and the buyer's 24/7 load profile.
Offtake Commitment Security:
Enables the buyer to fulfill minimum offtake commitments under the PPA by capturing excess midday energy that would otherwise be curtailed or sold at zero value.
Enhanced Bankability:
Transforms a variable VPPA into a "Firmed Product," increasing the contract's credit and making green financing disbursement significantly easier.

Solar Alone Stalls at One-Third of Demand
Even fully optimized, a solar-only system meets only
36% of the factory's load.
Why it plateaus
Under Decision 963 the priced peak shifts to 17:30 - 22:30  after sunset. Daytime panels offset only midday consumption.
The expensive evening peak stays on full price grid power.
Adding more panels doesn't help: past ~36% of load, midday surplus is curtailed or exported at low value,  so the optimizer stops at 3.45 MW.
It is a partial daytime hedge,  not a path to 24/7 clean energy.
